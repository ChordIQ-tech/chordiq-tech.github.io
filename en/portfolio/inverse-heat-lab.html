Thermal and inversion

Inverse Heat Lab

From a handful of thermocouple readings to the heat-transfer coefficient h, the whole field and the temperatures nobody measured.

The problem

h is the number every thermal calculation depends on, and nobody can measure it directly. Correlations typically promise ±25%. Thermocouples, on the other hand, are cheap.

How it works

A physics-informed neural network built with PINNeAPPle: you write the equation as text, h becomes a trainable parameter, and the network learns the field and h together. Every case comes with its complete script filled with your inputs: install the package, run it, get the same answer.

Measured results

  • 1D fin: h in about 20 s, cross-checked against the analytic solution
  • 2D plate: h = 14.8 ± 0.3 for a true 15; hot spot at 79.2 °C vs 79.1 °C from an independent finite-volume solver
  • 3D block: the chip's temperature, which no sensor reaches, comes out at 74.6 °C vs 74.5 °C, from thermocouples on top only
