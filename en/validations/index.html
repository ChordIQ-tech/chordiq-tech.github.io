Oil & gas · mining · pneumatic conveying

Erosion in a 90° bend: CFD validated against an open experiment

End-to-end simulation in OpenFOAM, compared against a published experiment, with an AI model trained on top.

1.05–1.49×simulated / measured erosion (target: factor of 2)
1.0%AI error on pressure drop, on new cases
Validateddetails ↓

The simulation landed between 1.05× and 1.49× of the erosion rate measured in the lab in the three experiments tested (Vieira et al., 2016), within the factor-of-2 criterion the industry uses to accept an erosion model. The traditional hand formula, with no CFD, came out about 100× below the measured value in the same regime. The full campaign, 32 simulations from geometry to trained model, ran in 23 minutes for less than R$0.30 of compute, and the resulting AI model answers in milliseconds, with pressure drop at 1% error on points never seen in training.

Power electronics · thermal optimization · product engineering

Surrogate model matches real 3D simulation, 227,000× faster

A trained neural network predicts the junction temperature of a heat sink in microseconds, checked every round against a real 3D FEniCS simulation.

227,059×faster than the real 3D simulation, on average
4.46%mean temperature error vs. 3D FEA (worst case: 7.35%)
Validateddetails ↓

Seven real heat-sink geometries, deliberately different from one another (dense, sparse, tall and short fins, high and low power), each checked against a real 3D finite-element simulation (dolfinx/FEniCS, mesh via gmsh), not against another AI model. The surrogate answered in microseconds where the real simulation took 0.6 to 5.9 seconds, with temperature error within 10% in all 7 cases. The same run was repeated three times on different days: the temperature prediction is deterministic (4.46%/7.35% identical every time); only the speed-up factor changes with the machine load at the time of the test.

0.05%velocity-field error vs. OpenFOAM
1.3%mean error on the coefficients (Cd, Cl, velocity)

Engineering verification · mechanical design

Monte Carlo reveals the real margin of an "approved" design

A brake disc passes the nominal test. The real manufacturing tolerance tells a different story.

495.9 °Cnominal (safety limit: 500 °C)
1.9 °Creal margin under manufacturing tolerance
Validateddetails ↓

On paper, the design had 4.1 °C of headroom to the safety limit. Propagating the real manufacturing tolerance (±0.05 mm in thickness) through 200 Monte Carlo parts, the 95th percentile reaches 498.1 °C, a real margin of only 1.9 °C. No part fails the test, but the confidence in the "approved" changes completely. This is exactly the kind of check we apply before any engineering decision leaves the screen for production.

Space robotics · agriculture · off-road machines

Rover mobility: the physical surrogate beats the reference solver

A published AI model failed physically. We diagnosed the problem, built a robust version and recommended not using it in production.

0.7–3.8%error against the reference solver
45×less traction error than the original model
Validateddetails ↓

The corrected version reproduces the traction force of the classical physical model (Bekker–Wong) with a traction error 45× smaller than the original published version, and three physical constraints are now guaranteed by construction, not only by training. The most honest part of the work came after: the cost analysis showed that, for this specific problem, a well-implemented classical numerical method is faster and more accurate than the AI itself, and we recommended that to the client, not the flashier path. Fluids lab · wind tunnel · R&D

Software PIV recovers a vortex field with 3.9% error

A synthetic particle-image pair, a vortex field with an exact analytical solution, processed by the same cross-correlation algorithm that runs in production.

3.94%mean relative error vs. the exact analytical field
81velocity vectors recovered in a single pass
Validateddetails ↓

Each synthetic particle was displaced by the exact rotation matrix of a rigid-body vortex, not by an image approximation, so the reference carries no discretization error of its own. Running the real cross-correlation algorithm (the same one that serves the production endpoint) on the image pair, the recovered vectors reproduce the rotational structure of the flow with 3.94% mean error and 16% at the worst point (near the vortex centre, where the true velocity tends to zero), with no camera hardware, laser or proprietary software, just video and processing.

Mesh converter removes 100% of the defects of a legacy export

A mesh with typical legacy-export defects (duplicate faces, degenerate faces, flipped normals) processed by the real conversion and repair endpoint.

25/25defective faces removed (duplicate + degenerate)
100%consistent normals after the repair
Validateddetails ↓

A real mesh was deliberately damaged the way legacy CFD/CAD exports usually arrive: 15 exact duplicate faces, 10 degenerate (zero-area) faces and 20 faces with flipped normals. Chemical processes · pharma · industrial mixing

Stirred-tank digital twin gets the known physical scaling law right

10 real OpenFOAM (MRF) simulations of a stirred tank, covering 15 to 190 rad/s, with the surrogate recovering the known power-vs-speed physics.

0.01%velocity-field error vs. OpenFOAM
1.9%mean error on the scalars (power, Re, TKE)
Validateddetails ↓

Ten real OpenFOAM runs with a moving reference frame (MRF) of a stirred tank, from 15 to 190 rad/s of impeller rotation. Physically, the sweep recovers power ∝ ω^2.7, very close to the classical law for turbulent stirred tanks (P ∝ ρN³D⁵), a sign that the surrogate learned the real physics of the problem, not just how to interpolate points. The predicted velocity field reproduces even the wake of each of the impeller's 4 blades, with 0.01% error.

Data-science teams · AI model operations

Training copilot diagnoses and fixes a broken model on its own

A physics-informed neural network launched with a bad configuration is automatically diagnosed and corrected in 4 rounds, with no human intervention.

100%→5%real error against the exact solution, in 4 rounds
17stotal diagnosis and correction time, on CPU
Validateddetails ↓

Round 0 learns nothing, 100% error against the exact solution of a Poisson problem with a known answer. An automatic diagnoser reads the loss history, classifies the cause (plateau, oscillation, imbalance between physics and boundary condition) and proposes the next configuration change. Four rounds later, with no human intervention, the real error drops to 5%, a reduction of 20×. It is the same logic, at scale, behind our operational copilot service: memory of past episodes guiding the next decision, with explicit confidence about what changed.

Automotive · structures · vehicle safety

A crash surrogate's confidence interval hits the statistical target

A crash energy-absorption surrogate, transferred between vehicle programs, with prediction intervals that really cover what they promise.

1.7–2.5%error after SUV → sedan transfer
94–100%real coverage of the promised 90% intervals
Validateddetails ↓

A surrogate trained on the physics of progressive collapse of thin-walled tubes (Abramowicz–Jones model) for an SUV program generalizes poorly to a sedan without adjustment (17–32% error, as expected when the regime changes), but after transfer fine-tuning with 180 sedan samples, the error drops to 1.7–2.5%. More importantly: the 90% prediction intervals built by conformal prediction really cover the true value between 94% and 100% of the time, not an invented confidence number: the same statistical honesty we apply in any verification before an engineering decision.
